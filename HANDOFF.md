## Decisions made while building (none contradict the design doc)

- TypeScript throughout. The engine's state and content types are the contract the renderer, UI, scripts and later the server all share; it is worth the stricter edits.
- Tailwind 3.4 (classic config file) rather than 4, to match the Deadblock setup you know.
- No `React.StrictMode`: its double-mounted effects would open two WebGL contexts on the one canvas in dev.
- Tiles are 64 px in board space and the board letterboxes into the canvas. The map is 12×16 (3:4); on a phone in portrait that leaves empty space above and below, so Phase 2 portrait maps should be taller (about 10×20).
- The sim stores `px, py` for every creep and projectile so the renderer interpolates between ticks; the sim rate is 20 Hz, the render rate is capped at 60 (30 with battery saver).
- Rootkin "Growth" (towers strengthen while standing) is not in the slice; `Tower.placedTick` is recorded so Phase 2 can add it without a save-format change.

## Balance notes (Phase 2 work, numbers are starting points)

- Kinetic vs Plated is 0.75, so the Rootkin opening against the Assembly is intentionally sluggish; Weepvine's bio DoT (1.5× vs Plated) is the counter. The scripted builder never buys Weepvine first, and a human who does should feel the difference.
- Waves 6–10 are where lives are lost (dozers at 460 hp, leak cost 3). Earlier waves are a tutorial.
- Bounties: 5 / 11 / 30, wave bonus 20, early-call bonus 1 sap per second left. Total sap over a perfect run is about 1,900.

## v0.7.2 — reachable pads, sign-up URL fix (2026-10-24, later)

- **Every build pad can now reach the path.** The normal and hard maps had pads 2.0–4.1 tiles from the nearest path cell; the shortest attack range in the game is 1.8 tiles, so towers there never fired. Rule now: a pad is at most 1.5 tiles (centre to centre) from a path cell — orthogonally or diagonally adjacent. 49 pads were moved to the nearest free cell that satisfies it (pad counts unchanged: 44 / 23 / 24 / 13), the validator fails the build on any pad beyond 1.5 (`PAD_REACH` in `scripts/validate-content.mjs`), and the backgrounds and layout guides were regenerated from the new grids (`gen-background.py`, `gen-guides.py`). The sim smoke still wins all 16 level × race pairs. Difficulty on the harder maps comes from fewer pads and longer stretches of path without them, not from pads that cannot fire.
- **"Invalid path specified in request URL" on Create account** is Supabase's gateway refusing a URL it does not route: it happens when `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` carries a path — typically the REST endpoint (`…/rest/v1`) or the Auth endpoint (`…/auth/v1`) copied from the Data API page instead of the bare project URL. `src/net/supabase.ts` now keeps only the origin of whatever was pasted (and warns in the console), and the login screen explains the fix if the message ever reaches it. `.env.example` and the guide say "bare project URL" in so many words. Fix your `.env` and the Netlify variable to `https://<ref>.supabase.co`, then redeploy with a cleared cache.

## v0.19.0 — the tilted camera and the lit ground (2026-10-28, night)

The board seen from an angle, StarCraft-style, with the painted map lit as relief and by the shots. **The camera** (`src/render/camera.ts`, new). A homography from board px (TILE units, y down the trail) to screen px: the far edge narrower, the whole board shorter, as a plane seen in perspective. `Camera.fit(boardW, boardH, width, height, tilt)` fits the board into the host (`scale = min(width / boardW, height / (boardH × tilt.height))`), sets the four screen corners (a trapezoid, inset `(sw × (1 − top)) / 2` on each side of the far edge) and solves the 3 × 3 homography and its inverse. `project(x, y)` gives the screen point and the local scale `s` (screen px per board px at that depth); `unproject` takes a screen point back to the board for picking; `place(cam, sprite, bx, by, bw, bh)` sets a sprite's position and size from them. Three tilts: `TILT_LANDSCAPE` {top 0.7, height 0.74} when the host is wider than 1.1 × its height, `TILT_PORTRAIT` {top 0.82, height 0.86} otherwise (phones are width-limited; a mild tilt keeps the far rows readable), `FLAT` {1, 1} when the camera setting is off — the flat case is the old fit-to-host scale exactly.

**Two groups** (`src/render/PixiRenderer.ts` v0.15.0). Everything lying on the ground — the painting, pad rings, aura and range ellipses, shadows, ground effects, hover and thread layers — draws in `groundRoot` in board px into a `RenderTexture`, which is shown through one `Mesh` on a `PerspectivePlaneGeometry` (2 × cols by 2 × rows vertices, corners from the camera) with the lit shader. Everything that stands up — towers (body and turret placed each frame, `zIndex` by row), creeps (body, hp bars, stun and boss bars, `zIndex` by projected y), projectiles, the particle and painted effects, the hp, cover and badge layers — draws in `board` in screen px, each sprite placed at its projected foot and scaled by `s`, so the far rows are smaller. `Effects` v0.7.0 and `PaintedFx` v0.2.0 take a `project` function and keep board coordinates for their motion; beams are drawn between projected ends. Ground ellipses (range, aura, shadow) are flattened to 0.78 of a circle under the tilt (0.52 flat, as before). The render texture's resolution tracks the camera (`cam.scale × min(dpr, 2)`, clamped 0.35–1.5: about one texel per screen px on the near edge) and is remade only when it moves by more than 25%; `destroy()` unbinds the textures from the shader before dropping them (Pixi warns otherwise). `cellToScreen` / `screenToCell` go through the camera, so picking, the placement ghost and the coverage numbers all land on the right cell.

**The engine** (`audio/compose/engine.py`): numpy/scipy synthesis at 48 kHz — drums (kick with pitch sweep and click, snare with body and snap, rim, clap, metallic hats, shaker, congas, woodblock, inharmonic metal for anvils and pipes, clicks and chitters, ride, toms, crash), a vinyl crackle, a sub bass, a Rhodes (tine and bell partials, tremolo), vibes, bells, a Karplus-Strong pluck for the muted guitar chucks, detuned-saw pads, strings and a flute; a sequencer on a 16th grid with swing on the off-16ths (0.56–0.61 by world), humanised timing (±4 ms) and velocities, pattern strings per bar (`X x o g .`), ghost notes and fills; buses (dusty drums: saturation, low-pass 8–13 kHz, EQ), a sidechain from the kicks on bass, keys and pad, a dub delay (dotted eighth, filtered feedback, ping-pong) and a reverb (synthetic IR) — both run *circularly* on loops, and notes are placed modulo the loop length, so every tail wraps to the start and **the seam is the music carrying on** (the composer's tails were the one real fault of their set); a true-peak limiter. **Mastering** (`master.py`): ffmpeg ebur128 measures, each piece is trimmed to the brief's target (−15 LUFS menu, −18 calm, −16 battle, −14 stings) under a −1.6 dBFS ceiling (true peaks landed at −2 to −5 dBTP: dynamic), then encoded OGG q6 + AAC 192k straight into `public/audio/music`. `npm run compose-music` runs all three steps and the manifest (needs numpy, scipy, ffmpeg).
